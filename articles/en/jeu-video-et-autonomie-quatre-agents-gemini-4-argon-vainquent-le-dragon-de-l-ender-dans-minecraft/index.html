Video game and autonomy: four Gemini 4 Argon agents defeat the Ender Dragon in Minecraft

Four Gemini 4 Argon agents have overcome Minecraft's challenges without imposed scenario, cooperating, writing code and correcting their mistakes.

Winning the Ender Dragon in Minecraft demands much more than fighting. We need to find resources, build the right equipment, explore unknown areas and prepare several steps before reaching the final boss. This is precisely what makes this experience interesting to test AI agent capabilities autonomous.

According to the information reported, four agents based on Gemini 4 Argon, attributed to Google DeepMind in the cited sources, were placed in a randomly generated Minecraft world. They had to start from scratch and reach the Ender Dragon without following a predefined sequence of actions.

The result announced is concrete: 27 hours and 14 minutes to complete the mission, with 2,783 programmes written and executed during the experiment. These programs were used to translate their decisions into actions in the game, rather than limiting the AI to a simple conversation describing what she would have done.

The challenge was not simply to chain commands. Officers had to advance in an environment where available resources, hazards and barriers imposed successive decisions. An error could compromise some of their progress and force them to reconsider their approach.

Collaboration was another central element. The four officers used Minecraft's cat to exchange information and, according to the reports cited, had a shared memory. They could thus coordinate their actions and retain elements useful for collective progress. The interest of this organization is to allocate a complex objective between several agents, rather than entrusting each step to an isolated system.

The 2,783 programs announced above all illustrate the importance of implementation in this type of system. Producing a convincing answer is not enough: an agent must transform his decisions into actions, observe the results and adjust the rest of his work. The time required to complete the mission also reminds us that autonomy does not necessarily mean speed.

For companies, the interesting question is whether this method can work in professional environments. Agents able to use software tools, assign tasks and resume a process after failure could help automate some computer operations. But success in Minecraft alone does not demonstrate that they can reliably manage real systems or sensitive tasks.

This experience should therefore be seen as a demonstration of capabilities, not as evidence that AI officers can already work alone without supervision. In order to measure their actual usefulness, it will be necessary to know the exact test conditions, possible human interventions, the cost of calculations and the possibility of replicating the result.
